A build plan for the Boxcode HR & operations suite — what MyCo does, what we do differently, and the one architectural decision that makes the gate hardware question stop mattering.

Stack · Next.js + FastAPI + PostgresScale · <60 staff · 1 office · IndiaCapture · mobile: geofence + face matchModules · 12 planned, 5 in phase 1

01
No gate hardware. The phone is the reader.

Decision made: attendance is captured by a small mobile app — GPS geofence plus a face match — exactly the way staff already punch in MyCo today. No biometric reader integration, because that's disproportionate effort for what is fundamentally a check-in.

The ingest layer was built vendor-neutral anyway, so this costs us nothing. The gate adapter still sits in the repo at app/adapters/zkteco_adms.py, unwired. If a reader is ever bought, we import it in one line and everything downstream is untouched.

The core decision that survived the pivot
Every way a person can mark attendance — phone, kiosk tablet, HR entering it manually, and one day maybe a gate reader — produces the same normalized punch event. Adapters translate; the core never knows the difference. That's why dropping the hardware was a one-line change rather than a rewrite.

How a punch is verified

Three checks, cheapest first, so we never pay for face recognition on a punch that was never going to count. Every attempt is stored including the failures — silently discarding a rejected punch is how an attendance dispute becomes unwinnable three months later.

CheckWhat it catchesWhat it costs

  |  1 · Device binding  |  One phone per employee. A colleague punching from their own handset is refused.  |  Free — a database lookup.

  |  2 · Geofence  |  Punching from home. Also rejects faked GPS, stale fixes, and readings too imprecise to mean anything.  |  Free — haversine, 15 lines.

  |  3 · Face match  |  Confirms the selfie is the person enrolled.  |  AWS Rekognition, about $3/month at your volume.

1:1, not 1:N. Because the employee is already logged in, we only ever ask "is this them?" — never "who is this?". That's more accurate, cheaper, and doesn't get worse as headcount grows. We store an S3 key for the reference photo and nothing else: no embeddings, no image bytes in the database. Biometric data is the last thing you want sitting in a table someone might dump to a laptop.

The pipeline

Capture

adapters · pluggable

Gate device (push)ZKTeco / eSSL speak ADMS — the device POSTs punches to a URL we host. No polling, no SDK.

Gate device (pull)Older units: a tiny on-prem agent reads TCP 4370 on a loop and forwards.

Mobile appSelfie + on-device face match + GPS geofence. For field and WFH staff.

Kiosk / QRTablet at reception, rotating QR. Zero hardware cost.

ManualHR regularization, always with actor + reason + audit row.

→

Raw event store

punch_events · append-only

Never edited, never deleted. Corrections are new rows, not overwrites.

Idempotent. Dedupe hash on device_sn + device_user_id + ts — a device replaying its offline buffer can't double-punch anyone.

Two clocks. Store device time and server receive time; readers drift and we need to prove which.

Partitioned monthly. 500 staff × 4 punches × 25 days ≈ 50k rows/month — trivial now, tidy at scale.

→

Resolver

attendance_day · recomputable

Pure function. Raw events + shift policy → one row per employee per shift-date. Re-runnable at any time.

Produces first-in, last-out, worked minutes, break minutes, late-by, early-out, OT, and a status.

Keyed by shift date, not calendar date — night shifts crossing midnight are one day, not two halves.

A late-arriving backdated event just re-triggers the resolver for that date. Nothing manual.

That resolver output is the single source of truth every other module reads: leave balances, payroll, overtime, the manager dashboard, the WhatsApp daily digest. Get it right once.

02
The traps in attendance that eat three weeks each

These are the reasons attendance modules ship late. Worth deciding now, not in month two.

TrapWhat goes wrongHow we handle it

  |  Phone offline  |  No signal in the basement car park; the punch is lost or arrives an hour later.  |  App queues locally and retries. Ingest accepts backdated punches forever and re-runs the resolver for affected dates.

  |  In/out direction  |  Two taps get read as two entries and the day resolves to nothing.  |  Infer by ordering within the shift window (odd = in, even = out); the app also shows the person which one they're about to do.

  |  Night shifts  |  A 22:00–06:00 shift lands as two days, each showing half a day worked.  |  Shift-date keying, with a configurable cutover hour per shift template.

  |  Timezones  |  Server in one region, office in another; DST silently shifts everyone's late-marks.  |  Store UTC always, plus tenant timezone. Never store naive local time.

  |  Regularization  |  Employee forgot to punch out; HR edits the record; three months later nobody can explain the payroll number.  |  Corrections are approval-workflowed events with actor, reason, timestamp. The raw log stays untouched.

  |  Buddy punching  |  Someone holds up a photo of a colleague, or punches from a friend's phone.  |  Face match + camera-only capture (no gallery picks) + one-phone-per-employee binding. No liveness detection in v1 — that's a known gap, and Rekognition Face Liveness is the upgrade if it ever becomes real.

  |  Fake GPS apps  |  Free Android apps spoof location trivially. Your geofence becomes decoration.  |  Android reports when a fix was mocked — we ask, and reject. Also reject fixes older than two minutes or accurate to worse than 100m.

  |  Payroll coupling  |  Attendance edits after payroll runs, and the payslip no longer matches the data.  |  Payroll runs snapshot attendance and lock the period. Later edits go to the next cycle as an adjustment line.

04
Modules — twelve planned, sequenced by what unblocks what

You picked all four groups. That's fine as a destination, but the order is not arbitrary: attendance is upstream of payroll, and the employee record is upstream of everything.

Phase 1 — the spine weeks 1–5

Auth & org setup
core

Org, locations, departments, roles, invite flow. Boring, blocking, do it first.

Employee directory
core

Profiles, org chart, documents, joining/exit. Every other module foreign-keys here.

Attendance
core

Ingest → raw events → resolver → daily records. Live gate feed, regularization, exception queue.

Shifts & rosters
core

Templates, assignments, week-offs, holiday calendar. Attendance is meaningless without it.

Leave management
near

Types, accrual, balances, approval chains. Consumes attendance, feeds payroll.

Phase 2 — money and paperwork weeks 6–10

Payroll
high effort

Salary structures, attendance-driven runs, payslip PDFs, period locking. The compliance rules depend on which country — see open questions.

Expenses & reimbursements
low effort

Claims, receipt upload, multi-level approval, payout batching.

Documents
low effort

Offer letters, ID proofs, policy acknowledgements, expiry reminders.

Phase 3 — work & sales weeks 11–16

Tasks & projects
medium

Assignments, daily activity reports, time-against-task.

CRM — leads & pipeline
medium

Lead capture, stages, activity log, quotations. Effectively a second product; scope it tightly.

Field tracking
medium

Background GPS pings, route playback, client visit check-in with geo-stamp, distance for travel claims.

Engagement & comms
light

Announcements, timeline, birthdays, visitor log, WhatsApp/email daily digest.

05
Stack & infrastructure

LayerChoiceWhy this one

  |  Web app  |  Next.js 15 App Router, TypeScript, Tailwind, shadcn/ui  |  Server components for the heavy tables; you already build this way.

  |  Data grid & charts  |  TanStack Table + Recharts  |  Attendance is fundamentally a big filterable grid. Don't hand-roll it.

  |  API  |  FastAPI, Pydantic v2, SQLAlchemy 2.0, Alembic  |  Typed contracts, auto OpenAPI → generate the TS client, no drift.

  |  Background jobs  |  Celery or ARQ + Redis  |  Resolver runs, payroll runs, digests, device health checks.

  |  Database  |  Postgres 16, RLS per org, monthly partitions on punch_events  |  One engine for relational, JSONB, and time-series-ish punch data.

  |  Files  |  S3 + presigned URLs  |  Punch selfies, receipts, payslips, ID documents.

  |  Realtime  |  SSE for the live gate feed  |  Simpler than WebSockets and enough for a one-way stream.

  |  Mobile  |  React Native (Expo)  |  The only way anyone punches. Also leave, expenses, payslips.

  |  Face verification  |  AWS Rekognition CompareFaces  |  1:1 against one enrolled photo. ~$3/month. No model to train, no GPU to run.

  |  Hosting  |  One small EC2 — Postgres + API + nginx  |  60 people generate ~2,600 punches a month. Splitting tiers would be operational work for no benefit.

Two things the server must get right
Postgres stays bound to localhost. A database listening on a public IP is how small companies get ransomed; the security group should allow 22 and 443 only. And the mobile app needs real TLS — iOS refuses plain HTTP outright, so a domain and a certbot certificate are prerequisites, not polish. The provisioning script in infra/ec2-setup.sh does both.

07
Open questions before I scaffold

Settled so far: mobile capture, Rekognition, one EC2, India, under 60 staff, Postgres. What's left:

The office coordinates and radius. Exact lat/lng of the building and how far out a punch may be. 150m is the placeholder; a large campus or a multi-floor building with poor GPS may need more.

Server details. Host, SSH user, key, and whether there's a domain pointing at it. iOS won't talk to a plain-HTTP API, so the domain isn't optional.

Who enrolls the face photos? Someone has to take 60 reference photos. Cleanest is HR uploading them from the admin panel; self-enrolment on first login is easier but means nobody verified the photo is really that person.

Internal tool or product? If Boxcode might sell this later, the multi-tenancy and white-labelling decisions get made now rather than retrofitted.

Payroll specifics. India means PF, ESI, professional tax, TDS and Form 16. That's phase 2, but the salary structure model should be built knowing it.

Built so far, in Documents/boxcode-hrms: the attendance schema, the shift resolver (pure function, 22 passing assertions covering night shifts, grace periods, missing punch-outs and weekly offs), the geofence checks (9 passing assertions covering spoofed GPS, stale fixes and useless accuracy), the Rekognition wrapper with a stub mode for offline development, the mobile punch endpoint, and the EC2 provisioning script.

Next: database migrations against the EC2 Postgres, auth, the Expo app's punch screen, and the admin attendance dashboard.
