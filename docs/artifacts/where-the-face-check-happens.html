Where the face check happens

Short answer: on the phone, not on a website. The dashboard has no camera and never touches a face. Here is the whole thing in one page.

Three pieces, one camera

You keep hearing "app", "dashboard" and "server" used loosely — mostly by me. They are three separate programs with three separate jobs, and only one of them has a camera.

MyCo works exactly the same way. It isn't one website doing everything either — it's a phone app your staff punch from, and a separate web portal HR logs into. We've built the same two halves.

One punch, in order

Krish taps Check In. The phone takes a selfie and reads its location, and sends both to the server. The server then asks three questions, cheapest first, and stops at the first "no".

What's real today

Questions 1 and 2 genuinely work. Question 3 is plumbed but not switched on — and the system says so rather than pretending.

CheckWorking?What happens now
Logged in Yes Everyone has an email and password. No login, no punch.
At the office Yes Punching from home is refused and tells you how far away you are. Faked GPS is caught.
Face matches Not yet The selfie is taken and saved, but nothing compares it. The punch is recorded as face not checked — never as a pass.

That last row is the part worth understanding. Nothing in your database claims a check that didn't happen, so when the comparison is switched on later, none of today's records become lies.

Switching question 3 on

Two things are needed, and neither is code we write ourselves — face recognition is a solved problem, and a hand-rolled one would be worse.

One photo per person. HR opens the enrolment page and uploads a single picture of each of the seven of you. Ten minutes, once. That's the reference every future selfie is compared against.

An engine to do the comparing. Either a model that runs on your own machine — free, offline, photos never leave the building — or AWS Rekognition, which is more accurate and costs a few dollars a month but needs an AWS account. Both plug into the same slot; switching between them is a one-line change.
