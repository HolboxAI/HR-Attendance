01

The problem, in one paragraph

Boxcode's ~60 staff currently mark attendance in MyCo. It works, but the interface is poor, the data lives in someone else's system, and nothing downstream — payroll, leave, reporting — can be built on top of it. We are replacing it with our own, starting with the one module everybody touches every single day.

02

"Office only" — what that actually costs

You want punching to work inside the office and nowhere else. That's the right requirement, but GPS alone cannot deliver it, and it's worth being precise about why before we pick a setting.

A phone knows where it is to within roughly 10–30m outdoors, and considerably worse indoors where the signal bounces off the building. So a radius tight enough to exclude the car park will also start rejecting people sitting at their desks. Every metre you tighten trades a false accept for a false reject — and a false reject means someone is marked absent for a day they actually worked.

The answer: office WiFi

To see your access point's BSSID — the router's MAC address — a phone has to be associated to that access point, which in practice means it's in the building. It can't be faked from home without physically cloning your router. It's a far stronger signal than GPS and it costs nothing.

The code supports three policies, per location, so we can start lenient and tighten once we see real data from the pilot:

PolicyBehaviourUse when
gps_onlyRadius check alone.No usable office WiFi. Weakest option.
wifi_or_gpsEither signal passes. On office WiFi, GPS is skipped entirely.Recommended start. Nobody is falsely rejected while we learn the building.
wifi_requiredMust be on the office network. Correct coordinates alone are refused.What you asked for. Switch to it after the pilot — one config change, no deploy.

One caveat worth knowing now: Android hands over the BSSID readily. iOS restricts it — reading it needs a special Apple entitlement, and the network name alone isn't good enough because anyone can name a phone hotspot "Boxcode-Office". If most staff are on iPhone, tell me and we'll request the entitlement early, since Apple review takes time.

The flow, end to end

This is the part that's been missing. Two flows: what a new joiner goes through once, and what everybody does every morning.

Onboarding — happens once per person

01

HR adds the employee

Name, employee code, phone, department, shift, joining date.

admin web · creates employee row, sends an invite SMS
02

Employee installs the app and sets a password

Invite link opens the app, they set a password.

mobile · issues a token, binds this phone to this employee
03

HR takes the reference photo

Deliberately HR and not self-service — somebody has to vouch that the face on file is really that person, or the whole face check is theatre.

admin web · uploads to S3, writes a face_enrollments row
04

App asks for camera and location permission

With a plain explanation of why. If either is refused, punching cannot work — the app says so rather than failing mysteriously later.

mobile · one-time OS prompts

Daily — what actually happens at 9:30am

01

Open the app

One screen, one big button: Check In. Below it, today's status and yesterday's hours. Nothing else competes for attention.

02

Tap it — the camera opens immediately

Front camera only, no gallery option. Location is captured silently in the same moment, so we never compare a selfie taken here with a position recorded somewhere else.

03

Three checks run, cheapest first

Is this the phone bound to this employee → are they in the office → is this their face. We stop at the first failure, so we never pay Rekognition for a punch that was never going to count.

api · ~2s on 4G
04

Confirmation, or a reason they can act on

"Checked in at 9:34am" — or "You're about 400m from the office". Never a bare "failed". Either way the attempt is recorded, including the rejection.

api · writes punch_events, queues the resolver
05

The resolver turns punches into a day

First in, last out, hours worked, late by, overtime, status. Runs on every punch and again overnight for anything that arrived late.

worker · writes attendance_day
06

HR sees it immediately

Live board of who's in, who's late, who hasn't shown. Exceptions — missing punch-outs, rejected attempts — collect in a queue rather than being silently swallowed.

admin web

When something goes wrong

01

Employee forgot to punch out

The day is flagged, not marked absent. They request a correction from the app with a reason.

02

Manager approves or rejects it

The original punches are never edited. The correction is a separate record with who asked, who approved, and why.

03

The day is recomputed

Not hand-edited. The same resolver runs again with the corrected input, so the number always matches the data behind it.

05

Acceptance criteria

A milestone is done when these pass, not when the code compiles. Written now so we can't move the goalposts later.

Punching

  • A punch from inside the office succeeds in under 8 seconds on office WiFi and on 4G.
  • A punch from home is rejected, and the employee is told the distance — not just "failed".
  • A rejected punch still appears in HR's view. Nothing is silently discarded.
  • Under wifi_required, correct coordinates on the wrong network are refused.
  • A faked GPS location is refused.
  • Someone else's phone cannot punch for an employee — the binding refuses it.
  • A photo of a colleague held up to the camera is refused by the face check. accepted gap in v1 — see below
  • A punch by someone with no enrolled photo is either refused or recorded as not checked — never as a passing check. fixed
  • Tapping check-in twice by accident does not create two records.
  • A punch made with no signal is queued on the phone and lands when signal returns.

The attendance record

  • A 22:00–06:00 shift resolves to one day, not two halves. passing
  • Arriving inside the grace period is not marked late. passing
  • A missing punch-out is flagged for correction, never silently marked absent. passing
  • A Sunday with no punches reads as weekly off, not absent. passing
  • Nobody is marked absent before their shift has ended.
  • Deleting every attendance_day row and recomputing produces identical results.
  • An HR correction leaves the original punches visible and attributed.

Admin

  • HR can see, on one screen, who is in, who is late and who is absent right now.
  • Every exception for the month can be cleared without touching the database.
  • Month-end attendance exports to Excel in one click.
  • Every change to an attendance record is attributable to a person and a time.

Milestones

M0 · Foundations

done

Schema, shift resolver, presence checks, storage and face interfaces. Every layer that would have to change for production sits behind a seam, so none of the prototype shortcuts are one-way doors.

M1 · It runs

done

SQLite at data/boxcode.db, the team seeded, ./run.sh bringing up API and dashboard together. No server, no AWS account, no Postgres install. Postgres remains one connection string away.

M2 · Punching works

done

Expo app with the check-in screen, camera and location captured in the same moment, three-stage verification, punches persisted and resolved into days. Rejected attempts stored with their reason rather than discarded.

M3 · HR can see it

done

Live board, stat tiles, exception queue, refused-punch log, per-employee month view, and a correction endpoint that adds a punch rather than editing one.

M4 · Face enrolment

done

Append-only enrolment records — re-enrolling supersedes, withdrawing deactivates — with quality checks at upload, because a bad reference photo silently poisons every comparison made against it afterwards.

M5 · Auth

done

Login, sessions, roles, and the phone bound to one employee. This now gates everything: until it exists, anyone can edit one line and punch as somebody else, and self-service enrolment cannot safely be offered at all.

M6 · Leave

done

Types, balances, accrual, approvals, holiday calendar — and, the point of the milestone, wired into attendance so an approved leave day stops reading as absent. HR edits every policy from the dashboard; a change never silently rewrites balances already earned.

M7 · Pilot with 5 people

next

Dual-run against MyCo for a week and explain every mismatch before anyone else moves across. Tighten to wifi_required based on what the building actually does. Only then does deployment become a question worth asking.

Not in v1, deliberately

Payroll, expenses, CRM, field tracking, tasks, gate hardware, a second office, and a web punch page. Every one of these is a real thing we want — and every one of them is a reason attendance ships late. They start after M6.

Liveness stays out of v1 — decided 27 Aug 2026, with the gap stated rather than left implied.

The acceptance criteria used to ask for a held-up photo to be refused while this list ruled out the only mechanism that does it. That was a contradiction, not a scope call. CompareFaces asks one question — is this the same face as the enrolled photo — and a printed photo or a face on a second screen answers it correctly. It never asks is this a live person. So that criterion has been struck.

What this actually leaves open. Not a stranger punching for someone — their face will not match. The open case is co-operative: an employee lends their bound phone to a colleague at the office along with a photo of themselves. Device binding and the geofence do not stop that, they only make it require physical collusion, on the victim’s handset, inside the building.

Why that is acceptable at this size. Seven people who all know each other, in one room. The cheat needs two willing participants and someone physically present anyway. Every punch selfie is stored, so if it does happen it is visible after the fact rather than invisible. Cost of accepting: nothing. Cost of the alternative: a per-check fee and a new SDK on the critical path of an 8-second flow.

When we do build it, we build it ourselves. Not AWS Face Liveness. A server-chosen challenge at punch time — blink, or turn your head one way — verified from a short burst of frames, which defeats a static print. Worth being honest that this is the achievable version: true passive liveness from a single still is an adversarial research problem, and a challenge-response scheme is still beatable by a prepared video replay. It raises the cost of cheating; it does not end it. Revisit at the pilot, on evidence.

Non-negotiables

AreaRequirement
Biometric dataNo face embeddings and no image bytes in the database. Only an S3 key for the reference photo. Punch selfies are deleted after 90 days; the reference photo goes when the employee leaves.
Database exposurePostgres bound to localhost. Security group opens 22 and 443 only.
TransportReal TLS on a real domain. iOS refuses plain HTTP outright, so this is a prerequisite, not polish.
AuditEvery mutation writes an audit row. Nothing is hard-deleted, ever.
BackupsNightly pg_dump to S3, 30-day retention, and a restore actually tested once before M6.
AvailabilityIf the API is down, the app queues punches locally and syncs later. Nobody loses a day's attendance to a deploy.
